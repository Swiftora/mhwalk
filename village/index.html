The Village

Eat & Shop on Main Street

Every storefront here is within a mile's walk of Old Town Hall

The tour walks past working storefronts the whole way. The buildings earned their stories; the businesses inside keep them open.

See it all on the tour map, with every stop and storefront pinned along Main Street.

Every Stop, In Walking Order

  1. 1Old Town HallBuilt on five-dollar shares, moved on rollers in 1920, home of the museum.1871 · 62 South Main Street
  2. AThe School That MovedSix schoolhouses on one lot since about 1720. One is now a house.24 and 26 Woodstown Road
  3. 2Friends Meeting HouseThe 1808 gable fell in a great wind. The meeting rebuilt and never left.1808 · South Main & Woodstown Road
  4. BThe Letters in the IronFour iron letters under a shop window, left by the chain that became Acme.c. 1925 · 54 South Main Street
  5. 3The Brick RowAlready standing in the town's earliest picture, 1844. Almost nothing has moved since.1771 · 42, 44 and 46 South Main Street
  6. 4Wolf Funeral HomeIn 1889 he advertised a Patent Corpse Preserver. Then the dead came to him.c. 1810 · 30 South Main Street
  7. 5The Harness Maker's Post OfficeFor thirteen years the whole post office fit inside a harness maker's house.c. 1840 · 26 South Main Street
  8. 6First Baptist ChurchEighteen founders, the county's first Baptist church, and Union soldiers at rest behind it.1845 · 18 South Main Street
  9. 7The Gaunt HouseA senator who answered the fire bell, and the sewing factory in his attic.c. 1885 · 14 South Main Street
  10. 8The WarehouseNearly a million bushels of sweet potatoes, and asparagus flown to Massachusetts in 1922.c. 1890 · 2 South Main Street
  11. 9The Old MillIn 1964 a tractor-trailer came through the wall. The feed counter never closed.1880s · 1 South Main Street
  12. CThe Bridge Under the BridgeUnder this road lie the piers of the prettiest bridge the village ever had.Raccoon Creek crossing
  13. N1Mullica HouseNamed for a family whose world vanished, except for this one little house.Early 1700s · 20 North Main Street
  14. N2Old Farmers National BankFour young men, everyone tied up, and 8,370 Depression dollars gone in 1932.1903 · 28 North Main Street
  15. N3The Union StoreEver wondered why Mullica Hill is an antiques town? Start with this building.1866 · 46 North Main Street
  16. N4St. Stephen's Episcopal ChurchThe town clock since 1880, and the congregation that brought its dead along.1853 · 51 North Main Street
  17. N5The Ware Law OfficeThe town's first lawyer, a chair-maker's son, and the tiny office behind his house.1850 · 53 North Main Street
  18. 10The Foundry That Turned AroundThe foundry that turned to face another street. Walk around back to find it.1857 · South Main & East Church Streets
  19. 11Trinity Methodist ChurchTwo churches in twenty-one years, and a parsonage from the town's first car dealer.1848 · South Main Street
  20. 12The Stratton HouseHe kept the bullet that took his leg, and showed it from this porch.1848 · 39 South Main Street
  21. 13Old Eagle HotelA tavern four years older than the United States, where the township was born.Est. 1772 · 51 South Main Street
  22. DThe Spicer HouseThis side of the creek was Spicerville once, and this was Spicer's house.Mid-1700s · 76 South Main Street
